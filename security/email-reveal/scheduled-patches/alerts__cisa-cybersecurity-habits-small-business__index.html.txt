<main><div class="alerts-hero"><div class="alerts-wrap"><a class="alerts-back" href="/alerts/">← All Alerts &amp; Insights</a><span class="alerts-eyebrow">SMALL BUSINESS GUIDE · CYBERSECURITY BASICS</span><h1>Four CISA cybersecurity habits every small business can start today</h1><p>Security doesn't have to begin with expensive tools. Consistent employee habits help prevent avoidable incidents.</p></div></div><article class="alerts-article"><div class="alerts-wrap alerts-article-inner"><p class="alerts-byline">By Suncoast Tech Support · Published December 3, 2026 · Small-business cybersecurity</p><p>CISA's <strong>Secure Our World</strong> campaign encourages four everyday practices that can reduce online risks: recognizing and reporting phishing, using strong passwords, turning on multifactor authentication (MFA), and updating software. These habits are relevant to small professional offices, storefronts and teams that depend on email, shared files and internet connections.</p>
<h2>1. Recognize and report phishing</h2>
<p>Before opening an unexpected invoice or account-warning link, check the complete sender address and verify urgent requests using an established phone number or an official application. Give employees a simple process to report suspicious messages without fear of blame. See our <a href="/alerts/business-email-compromise/">fake invoice and business email scam guide</a>.</p>
<h2>2. Use strong, unique passwords</h2>
<p>Create long, unique passwords for business accounts instead of reusing the same password across email, banking and vendor portals. A reputable password manager can help team members manage these passwords without writing them on sticky notes or sharing them in email.</p>
<h2>3. Turn on MFA wherever possible</h2>
<p>Use MFA on Microsoft 365, cloud file sharing, financial applications and administrative portals. Where available, prefer phishing-resistant options. Make sure recovery methods and backup access are documented so business operations can continue if a device is lost.</p>
<h2>4. Update software and retire unsupported devices</h2>
<p>Keep operating systems, browsers, routers, firewalls and applications current. Assign one person or provider to track updates, verify successful installation, and plan upgrades for older devices that no longer receive security patches.</p>
<div class="alerts-caution"><p><strong>Start with what matters most:</strong> Protect email and financial accounts first, identify where customer information is stored, and make one employee responsible for reporting suspicious activity. These measures complement, rather than replace, backups and incident response.</p></div>
<h2>Make these habits routine</h2>
<p>A monthly 15-minute review can help: check new employees and terminated accounts, verify MFA coverage, confirm backup success, review missing updates and discuss one real phishing example. Suncoast can support this process through <a href="/business-security.html">small-business cybersecurity reviews</a> and <a href="/managed-it/">monthly IT support</a>.</p>
