Fake tech support alerts: what small-business employees should do

An alarming computer warning or unexpected support call does not automatically mean a device is infected.

By Suncoast Tech Support · Published October 28, 2026 · General business guidance

Scammers can impersonate a software company or help desk through an unexpected phone call, message or browser pop-up. The goal may be to collect payment, persuade someone to install remote-access software, or obtain access to business information.

What employees should do

  1. Do not call the number inside an unexpected warning. Close the suspicious browser window if possible. A webpage cannot reliably diagnose a computer infection just by displaying a message.
  2. Do not grant unsolicited remote access. Use your company's established IT contact or support channel instead of accepting instructions from an unexpected caller.
  3. Do not share passwords or verification codes. Legitimate support should not need an employee to disclose an MFA code to an unknown caller.
  4. Report the message promptly. Note what appeared, which computer was involved, and whether anything was clicked, downloaded or installed.
  5. Use an independently verified support contact. Look up the official number or contact your existing IT provider using details already known to the company.

If remote access was already granted: Stop the session, disconnect the affected device from the network if doing so is safe, and contact your trusted IT provider. From a trusted device, secure potentially affected accounts. Notify the bank promptly if financial accounts or payment information may have been exposed.

What a small office can prepare in advance

Give staff a single verified IT support contact, a short reporting procedure, and guidance about browser warnings and MFA requests. Regular security updates, backups and appropriate account permissions also reduce preventable problems.
