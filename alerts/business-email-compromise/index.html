Fake invoices and business email scams: five checks before you pay

Before your office changes a vendor's bank details or pays an urgent invoice, make sure the request is genuine.

By Suncoast Tech Support · Published October 28, 2026 · General business guidance

Business email compromise (BEC) is a type of fraud where a message appears to come from a trusted employee, customer or vendor. A criminal may use a lookalike email address, impersonation, or access to a real account to request payment or sensitive information.

For a small office, the most important control may be a reliable process for verifying money-related changes—not just another security tool.

Five checks to make before you pay

  1. Verify changed payment instructions independently. Call your vendor using a phone number already in your records, not a number supplied in the unexpected email.
  2. Examine the sender's complete address. Look for misspellings, unfamiliar domains or changes in the email thread. A familiar display name is not proof.
  3. Pause when a request is unusually urgent. Scammers may pressure staff to bypass normal approvals, especially near payroll, closing dates or vendor deadlines.
  4. Use a second approval for sensitive payments. Have another authorized person confirm new bank details or exceptional transfers before funds leave the business.
  5. Make verification easy for employees. Document how to flag suspicious requests and reassure staff that checking with a manager is the correct response.

If money has already been sent: Contact your bank immediately, ask about recalling or stopping the transfer, and report the incident to the FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center. Preserve the email, payment details and other evidence.

Where technology support fits

Payment verification is an internal business process. IT support complements it by reviewing account access, suspicious sign-ins, MFA configuration, email rules and workstation security where appropriate.
